Synthesized slopes and offsets:

- Spring rate vs weight (the real weight axis): ~3 N/mm shock per ~10 kg (22 lb) band (KTM factory bands: 42/45/48 N/mm on the 2019 450 SX-F; 39/42/45 N/mm on the 2022 250 SX-F). ExxtremeMX independently gives ~0.1 kg/mm shock and ~0.01 kg/mm fork per ~15 lb band. Direction: heavier rider = stiffer spring. Confidence: high (two factory manuals plus one tuner table agree).
- Clicks per 10 lb of rider weight: effectively ZERO from reputable tuners. The cross-shop consensus (Factory Connection, Enzo, ExxtremeMX) is that rider weight is compensated with springs, not clickers. Modeling a nonzero "clicks per pound" slope would contradict stated professional practice. Direction is therefore: move the rider to the next spring band, keep clickers at baseline. Confidence: high.
- Skill / ability offset (the clicker axis): faster / more aggressive riders run stiffer and slower damping; slower / vet riders run softer and faster. Rough magnitude, from MXA quoting its own test riders on one bike: a compression spread of about 5 clicks across the speed range (e.g. 7-12 out). Direction: novice/C = more clicks OUT (softer); expert/A/pro = more clicks IN (stiffer). Confidence: medium (rests on Bones Bacon/Pro Circuit and MXA/PulpMX, qualitative more than tabulated).
- Air pressure vs weight: ~2 psi is about one spring rate (Keefer Inc). Stay within ~1 psi of the stock/base pressure unless the rider is very light or very heavy.
- Explicit "skill moves springs or clickers?" statements: tuners consistently separate the two axes: weight to springs; skill/speed/aggression to valving and clickers (toward stiffer/slower for faster riders). No reputable source recommends changing spring rate for skill alone, and none recommends changing clickers to carry weight.

Recommended engine implementation: treat weight as a spring-band selector (with the KTM/Sherco N/mm bands above as the template and ~3 N/mm shock per 22 lb as the default slope for platforms without a published table), and treat skill as a clicker offset of roughly -2 clicks (stiffer) per class step from C to B to A on compression, mirrored with a smaller rebound change, capped so it never substitutes for a spring change.

## Sub-task 3: adjuster locations by family

Count convention (all families): every manual and tuner guide reviewed counts clicks OUT from fully closed (turn clockwise to the stop, then back off N clicks). Teknik Motorsport's Offroad Suspension Setup Guide states this verbatim: "Note: All clicker settings are referred to from the fully hard (clockwise) position."

- KYB SSS 48 (Yamaha YZ125 / YZ250 / YZ250F / YZ450F): compression adjuster on top of the fork cap; rebound adjuster at the bottom of the fork leg (base bolt). Both legs are symmetric twin-chamber units carrying both spring and damping. From 2024 model year the fork compression adjuster is hand-adjustable (toolless) on the top cap (Dirt Rider); the shock LSC still needs a flat-blade screwdriver until the 2026 YZ450F adds a hand knob. This REFUTES the existing draft row that says compression is at the bottom: compression is at the top cap, rebound at the bottom. Sources: KYB OEM parts (rebound "bottom adjuster clicker nut"; REP hand-comp adjuster for 2023+ YZ250F/YZ450F), Dirt Rider. Tag: factory/tuner.
- KYB SSS Kawasaki 2020+ KX250/KX450, Beta KYB, Sherco KYB: same layout, compression on the top cap, rebound at the bottom of the leg, count from closed. Tag: inferred (shared KYB SSS design).
- KYB PSF-2 (Honda CRF450R 2013-2016): air valve on the fork cap; the fork carries high- and low-speed compression AND high- and low-speed rebound clickers (Keefer Inc). Exact per-leg positions not found. Tag: inferred.
- Showa 49mm coil (Honda CRF250R/450R 2017+): compression on top of the fork cap, rebound at the bottom of the fork leg. On the shock, the adjuster cluster moved from the LEFT side to the RIGHT side for the 2022 CRF250 generation (MXA 2022 CRF250). Count from closed. Source: MXA. Tag: tuner.
- Showa SFF-2 / SFF coil (Kawasaki KX250F through 2019): all damping is in the LEFT leg (compression on top of the left cap, rebound at the bottom of the left leg); the RIGHT leg holds the coil spring with a preload clicker on the right cap. Source: MXA 2015/2016 fork guides. Tag: factory/tuner.
- Showa SFF-Air TAC (KX450 2016-2018): three air chambers: inner chamber valve on top of the left fork cap, outer chamber bleed/Schrader on the left cap, balance chamber valve under the left fork lug. All damping is in the RIGHT leg (compression top-right, rebound bottom-right). Source: MXA. Tag: factory/tuner.
- Showa shock (Honda): LSC and HSC on the reservoir; rebound at the bottom/clevis. HSC is in turns (3 turns total range), LSC and rebound in clicks. Cluster moved left-to-right in the 2021/2022 redesign. Source: MXA. Tag: tuner.
- Showa BFRC shock (Suzuki RM-Z 2018+): compression and rebound adjusters are both on the piggyback reservoir, labeled "Com" (compression) and "Ten" (rebound); there is NO rebound adjuster under the shock and NO high-speed compression adjuster at all. Both are bleed-screw TURN adjusters, not clicks (MXA and MX-Tech: "the adjusters feature only turns and no clicks"). BFRC convention: count turns from closed, LSC about 1-1.5 turns out, rebound about 1-3 turns out. Sources: MXA, MX-Tech. Tag: factory/tuner.
- Ohlins TTX forks and shocks: fork cap layout: air bleed on the left (Torx T25), compression in the center (3mm Allen), rebound on the right (Torx T25). Shock: rebound knob at the bottom (hand-adjustable); compression/HSC on the reservoir. An HSC clicker was added on the TTX Flow DV shock for 2023. Source: MXA "RD Suspension-Tuned Ohlins". Tag: tuner.
- Sachs forks and shocks (Beta 2019 and earlier, older Sherco): open-cartridge fork with compression and rebound both counted from fully closed on the fork tops; ZF shock with LSC and rebound in clicks and HSC in turns. Sources: Beta (betausa.com) and Sherco (ManualsLib) Sachs manuals. Tag: factory.
- KYB shocks (Yamaha): LSC = small center clicker on the reservoir-top adjuster; HSC = large outer hex knob on the reservoir top; rebound = adjuster at the bottom clevis. HSC in turns (1/4-turn steps), LSC and rebound in clicks. Source: standard KYB layout, Keefer Inc. Tag: tuner.

## Sub-task 4: contested track-condition tuning rules

### 4a. Rutted hardpack: fork rebound faster or slower? Should compression change?

Source tally. Faster fork rebound (clicks OUT): Keefer Inc ("On hard pack dirt it is better to adjust the rebound faster while still maintaining control and bottoming resistance"); PulpMX ("a softer setting or a faster rebound can be used... I usually speed up rebound first to see if that helps the rear of the bike track better over hard bumps"); MXA (hardpack traction guidance); Vital MX rider threads. Count: 4. Slower rebound plus more low-speed compression (clicks IN): Teknik Motorsport offroad guide ("add 1-2 clicks of rebound and as the track gets rough, add 1-4 clicks of compression... to keep the front end from packing"); MXA rut-hold-up note. Count: 2.

Consensus: FASTER fork rebound on rutted hardpack (about 4:2). It flips toward slower rebound / more low-speed compression when ruts are deep and the fork deflects or packs, or at high off-road speed where hold-up dominates (Teknik's packing argument). Compression usually stays at baseline for hardpack ruts; only add LSC if the front packs or deflects out of the rut. The app's current rule (speed fork rebound one click) is SUPPORTED. Recommended app rule: Rutted hardpack: fork rebound +1 click out (faster). If the fork deflects/packs out of the rut, instead add +1 click shock LSC (or fork compression) rather than slowing rebound.

### 4b. Watered track that dries and gets choppy: firm the compression back up, or keep it soft?

Source tally. Keep it soft / go slightly softer plus faster rebound as it dries and roughens: Vital MX "end of the day" thread ("a couple clicks softer compression on both ends" on a dry, choppy track); Keefer Inc (square-edge chop wants softer comp and faster rebound); PulpMX (speed rebound / soften LSC for acceleration-chop traction); Click Suspension. Count: 4. Firm back up / add compression: Teknik offroad guide (add compression as the track roughens to stop packing); some MXA hold-up advice. Count: 2.

Consensus: do NOT simply firm all the way back up. As a watered track dries and gets rough/choppy, most MX tuners keep compression soft (or go slightly softer) and speed up rebound for compliance and traction between practice and the motos. It flips toward adding compression/hold-up when speed is high or the bike starts bottoming. Pro-mechanic pattern: plush/soft for morning traction, then favor faster rebound and softer LSC as it squares off, rather than re-firming compression. Recommended app rule: after morning traction softening, hold compression soft as the track dries. If it gets choppy: fork rebound +1 out and shock LSC -1 (softer). Only firm compression if the rider reports bottoming.

### 4c. Track that gets rough / square-edged over the day: firm compression to hold up, or soften for compliance?

Source tally. Soften compression (clicks OUT): Vital MX rough-track threads (multiple posters: "a couple clicks softer compression on both ends"); Keefer Inc (square edges want softer comp / faster rebound); PulpMX; Click Suspension. Count: 4. Firm compression (clicks IN) to hold the bike up: Teknik offroad guide (add compression to prevent fork packing); MXA hold-up advice; Troll Training (stiffer to ride higher in the stroke so the bike feels less harsh). Count: 3.

Consensus: LEAN SOFTER on compression for square-edge braking bumps on an MX track (about 4:3, genuinely close and the most contested of the three). It flips to FIRMER at high speed, for heavier/faster/expert riders, for off-road/desert, and whenever the fork is bottoming or packing low in the stroke, where riding higher in the stroke via more compression is the right call. The app's current rule (firm fork compression one click at a mid-day retune) CONTRADICTS the MX majority for the typical square-edge-chop case. Recommended app rule: rough/square-edged MX track: soften fork compression 1 click out (reverse the current firm-one-click default). Keep a firm +1 click only when speed is high, the rider is heavy/expert, the discipline is off-road/desert, or bottoming is reported.

## Conflicts between sources

- Rough-track compression direction (4c): MX tuners (Keefer, PulpMX, Vital MX, Click) say soften; off-road/high-speed and hold-up sources (Teknik, MXA, Troll Training) say firm. Resolved as speed- and discipline-dependent; MX default = softer.
- Rebound in ruts / rough (4a, 4b): Keefer and PulpMX say faster; Teknik says slower / add compression to prevent packing. Resolved as fast-rebound default, slow only when packing/deflecting.
- KYB SSS compression adjuster location: the existing app draft says bottom of the leg; verified manual/OEM-parts data says compression on the top cap, rebound at the bottom. Verified answer overrides the draft.
- Showa BFRC shock behavior: unlike every other shock in the catalog, it uses continuous bleed-screw turns (no clicks) and has no high-speed compression adjuster. The engine must special-case BFRC so it does not treat "turns" as "clicks" or expect an HSC value.
- KTM shock spring bands shift by model/year: the 2019 450 SX-F manual lists 42/45/48 N/mm across the 65-75 / 75-85 / 85-95 kg bands, while the 2022 250 SX-F manual lists 39/42/45 N/mm across the same bands. Use the model-year-specific manual, not a single global table.
